# Northcue check, Romanian

For a native speaker. About 45 minutes. No technical knowledge needed,
and please do not look anything up: your first reading is the answer.

Northcue turns confusing official letters into short calm cards for
people who find that post stressful. The reader may be frightened and
holding a letter about money they do not have. The app must never order
them around in its own voice. It may only report what the letter
demands, reassure, and suggest what to check. Your job in Part 1 is to
tell us which sentences sound like orders.

**Please finish Part 1 before reading Part 2 or Part 3.** The later
parts give away what the app watches for, and Part 1 only works if you
have not seen that yet.

## Part 1. The sentences, one mark each

Imagine each sentence printed on your card after you upload a letter.
Write one letter in the last column:

- **A** if the sentence itself orders YOU to do something. It tells you
  that you must pay, contact, send, return, confirm, or hand something
  over.
- **B** if it does not order you. It reports what the letter says,
  states a fact or a date, reassures you that nothing is needed, asks a
  question, or suggests something you could check.
- **C** if you are not sure.

Go with your first reading. If a sentence sounds like an order to you,
mark A even if a grammar book would disagree. And if any sentence
sounds rude, frightening, or machine-made, put a star beside it too.

## Part 2. Say it your way

Translate these five sentences the way an official UK letter written in
Romanian would put them to you. Not word for word: how a council or
government letter would actually say it.

1. You must pay £74.22 by 1 July 2026.
2. You must contact the council before 3 September 2026.
3. You must return the signed form.
4. You must confirm your identity within 30 days.
5. You must send your income information by 24 June 2026.

## Part 3. The word lists

These are the words the app watches for. Cross out any that no
official letter would use. Add any that are missing, especially the
ways letters say pay, contact, send, return, and confirm.

**Action words we watch for:** pl[ăa]t, achit, contact, sun, telefon, apel, r[ăa]spund, trimi[țts], trimis, furniz, confirm, verific, particip, prezent, prezint, complet, return, înapoi, depun, depus, ac[țt]ion, eliber, p[ăa]r[ăa]s, elimin, aranj, program, solicit, aplic, efectu, f[ăa]c, fac, pred

**Ways of saying must:** trebuie, va trebui, ar trebui, este necesar, este obligatoriu, se impune, sunte[țt]i obliga[țt]

**Reporting phrases:** scrisoarea indică, documentul prevede, conform, potrivit, avizul precizează, notificarea indică

## For reference only, nothing to do here

From the lists above the app generates about 736 word forms mechanically, so nobody typed each one and you are not being
asked to read them. You are checking the lists they grow from, which
is why Part 3 is short.

## What happens with your answers

Every mark you make is compared with what the app already believes.
Where you agree, that sentence is locked in as checked by you, with
your name and the date. Where you disagree, a person looks at that
sentence and the app changes or the record explains why not, in
writing, before your language is switched on. Nothing you write here
is published anywhere.
