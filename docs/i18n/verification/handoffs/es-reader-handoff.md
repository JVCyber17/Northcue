# A favour for Spanish readers, from Northcue

Northcue is a small UK tool that turns confusing official letters,
bills and government post into short, calm cards, for people who find
that post stressful: parents of neurodivergent children, older people,
people reading English as a second language. It now speaks Spanish,
and before that goes live, a real Spanish speaker needs to check
the sentences below. You are that person, and you are the first one.

Here is what your hour or two protects. The app must never order a
frightened reader around in its own voice. It may report what a letter
demands, reassure, and suggest what to check, but the difference
between "you must pay this now" and "the letter asks for a payment"
is the difference between panic and calm, and only someone who truly
speaks Spanish can hear it. That is the one question below: which
of these sentences ORDER the reader to do something?

## What to do

Read each numbered sentence as if it appeared on your own screen after
you uploaded a letter. Go with your first reading, and please do not
look anything up. You only need to note THREE kinds of row:

- the rows that ORDER you to do something (pay, contact, send,
  return, confirm, hand something over),
- the rows where you are genuinely UNSURE,
- and any row that sounds rude, frightening, or like a machine wrote
  it, whatever else it is.

Every row you do not mention counts as information, which needs no
note at all. Most rows are information, so this is quicker than it
looks.

## How to send your answers

Reply by message, in any form you like. All that is needed is:

1. The row numbers that order the reader to do something.
   For example: "Orders: 3, 12, 27".
2. The row numbers you were unsure about. "Unsure: 40".
3. Any rows that sounded wrong in tone, with a word about why.

That is the whole job. If you have five more minutes, one bonus that
helps more than anything: write, in Spanish, how an official UK
letter would tell someone "You must pay £74.22 by 1 July 2026" and
"You must contact the council". Your own wording, not a translation.

Thank you. Nothing you write is published anywhere; it is checked
against the app's behaviour, and where you and the app disagree, the
app is the one that changes or explains itself.
